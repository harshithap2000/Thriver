# Thriver research

> _Beyond Cancer. Into Life. Clinical care meets everyday wellness._

Thriver is an AI-powered aftercare companion for cancer survivors moving from clinical treatment to
long-term wellness: personalised exercise and nutrition by cancer type, surgery and days since the
last procedure, plus community and specialist access. **Launch market: India.**

Research round 1 (October 2026), compiled from web sources. Every claim links to its source; where
sources disagree or are weak, that is flagged. **Nothing here is legal or medical advice.** Verify
key facts with primary sources, a regulatory lawyer and clinical advisers before relying on them.

## The ten things to know

1. **The core idea is well supported.** Major guidelines (ACSM 2019, ASCO 2022) say exercise is safe
   for most survivors and improves fatigue, anxiety, depression, physical function and quality of
   life. "Avoid inactivity" is the baseline message. → [01](01-clinical-evidence.md#1-the-evidence-base-for-exercise)
2. **The precise promise is not.** Guidelines give general doses (about 30 min moderate aerobic
   3×/week plus resistance 2×/week), not validated day-by-day plans per cancer type and surgery.
   Early post-surgery limits are set by each surgical team. Thriver should personalise **within
   clinician-approved phases**, not invent post-op timelines. → [01](01-clinical-evidence.md#4-what-this-means-for-thrivers-personalisation-promise)
3. **India has a real, documented gap.** About 1.57 million new cancer cases a year (2025 estimate).
   Cancer rehabilitation is described in the literature as "underdeveloped and underutilised", and
   Indian exercise-oncology research is thin. → [02](02-market-and-competitors.md#1-the-problem-in-india)
4. **Money is the hardest constraint.** In a 12,148-patient Indian study, 59% of hospitalised cancer
   patients faced catastrophic health spending once indirect costs were counted. Charging patients
   directly will be hard; hospitals, insurers, pharma and CSR are the likelier payers. → [03](03-business-model-and-funding.md#2-who-pays)
5. **No Indian player owns "post-treatment rehab + daily wellness" digitally.** ZenOnco (integrative
   care, community app), Onco.com (navigation), HCAH (in-person rehab) and pharma programmes like
   Roche's Blue Tree each cover part of it. → [02](02-market-and-competitors.md#3-competitors)
6. **Check the name.** A US cancer app called **Thrivors** offers exercise, nutrition and mental
   wellness. Do a trademark search in India and the US before investing in the brand. → [02](02-market-and-competitors.md#4-name-check-thrivors)
7. **The AI exercise prescription may be regulated software.** India's medical-device rules already
   cover software with a medical purpose, and CDSCO issued draft software guidance in October 2025.
   Positioning, claims and features decide which side of the line Thriver falls on. → [04](04-mvp-and-regulation.md#21-medical-device-software-cdsco)
8. **Data protection deadlines are close.** The DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025, with phased
   start dates (one source puts some obligations at 13 November 2026, the bulk by May 2027). Health
   data needs consent-first design now. → [04](04-mvp-and-regulation.md#22-data-protection-dpdp-act-2023--rules-2025)
9. **Ratan Tata passed away in October 2024**, and the UC-RNT fund he backed is reported to be winding
   down. Pitch the Tata ecosystem through Tata Trusts or Tata Memorial collaborations instead. → [03](03-business-model-and-funding.md#5-the-tata-question)
10. **Investors exist for this.** India-focused early-stage health funds include W Health Ventures,
    HealthQuad, Endiya, Chiratae and 3one4, among others. Indian healthtech raised about US$828M in
    H1 2025. → [03](03-business-model-and-funding.md#4-investors)
