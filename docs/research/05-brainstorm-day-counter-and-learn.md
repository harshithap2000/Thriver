# 05 – Brainstorm: "days since surgery" on Home, and a Learn section

Brainstorm notes (October 2026), lightly researched. Ideas are options to choose from, not decisions.

---

## Part A: Showing days since surgery on Home

### What the research says to be careful about

- **Milestones are emotionally mixed.** Survivors describe "cancerversaries" as joy and relief mixed
  with anxiety. An **"anniversary effect"** can trigger stress around significant dates, and some
  survivors feel guilt about celebrating when friends did not survive.
  ([CURE](https://www.curetoday.com/view/oct-12-as-i-approach-my-cancerversary-i-don-t-feel-like-celebrating),
  [LBBC](https://www.lbbc.org/blog/what-makes-a-cancerversary),
  [Testicular Cancer Society](https://www.testicularcancer.org/testicular-cancer-101/understanding-cancerversary-guide))
- **There's no right way to mark a date, and skipping is fine.** So the counter must be optional,
  and the person chooses what it counts.
- **Recovery has real, general landmarks** (mastectomy example): drains out within days to weeks;
  most usual activities around **3 weeks**; wounds fully healed around **6 weeks**; driving when an
  emergency stop is comfortable (often about 3 weeks); work often after **4–8 weeks**. The person's own
  surgical team decides.
  ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/mastectomy/), [HSE](https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/mastectomy))

### Design principles

1. **The person chooses what to count:** days since surgery, since finishing treatment, since "no
   evidence of disease", since joining Thriver, or nothing at all.
2. **Gentle by default:** "Week 4 of your recovery" can feel lighter than a raw number. Switch from
   days to weeks to months as time passes (for example after day 100).
3. **Growth, not streaks:** no "you broke your streak" guilt. Missed days never take progress away.
4. **Milestones invite support, not just confetti:** on big days, offer a calm session, a journal
   prompt, or a nudge for family to send notes.
5. **Handle setbacks with care:** recurrence, new treatment or another surgery should allow "start a
   new chapter", keeping the history rather than resetting it.

## Part B: A "Learn" section with expert articles and survivor stories

### What the research says

- **Survivor stories can help.** Small studies suggest survivor-led storytelling can improve
  **self-efficacy, resilience and quality of life**: a randomised trial in post-operative lung cancer
  patients, plus pilots. Evidence is promising but thin, and survivor testimony is being tested as a
  way to encourage exercise.
  ([X-MOL / Eur J Oncol Nurs](https://ma.x-mol.com/paper/1760534329096376320),
  [ASU pilot](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/storytelling-intervention-for-patients-with-cancer-part-2-pilot-t),
  [cancer.fr](https://cancer.fr/professionnels-de-sante/veille/nota-bene-cancer/bulletin-n-242/understanding-narrative-effects-in-physical-activity-promotion-the-influence-of-breast-cancer-survivor-testimony-on-exercise-beliefs-self-efficac))
- **Survivors are willing to be educators:** 79% of breast cancer patients and survivors in one study
  felt able to share their stories with others.
  ([PMC9906524](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9906524/))
- **Content sourcing:** US **National Cancer Institute text is free of copyright** and can be reused
  with credit (graphics vary; the PDQ name and logo have rules; NCI suggests linking because content is
  updated). ([cancer.gov](https://www.cancer.gov/policies/copyright-reuse)) Most other publishers
  (Macmillan, Cancer Research UK, hospital sites) are copyrighted, so link to them or partner.
- **India has survivor-story sources but no single dedicated platform:** hospital programmes (Apollo
  **CanWin**, Manipal **We Can**, HCG with **Pink Hope / Self V**), peer groups (**Anandi Sheroes**),
  the Indian Cancer Society and media features.
  ([UNI / CanWin](https://test.uniindia.com/apollo-cancer-centres-launches-canwin/south/news/3498609.html),
  [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fkarnataka%2Fbengaluru%2Fgritty-survivors-recount-their-fight-2092920),
  [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/140259/stories-cancer-survivors-battle-disease))
  **That gap is an opportunity for Thriver.**

### Ideas

**1. Structure: two strands in one section, "Learn"**

- **Guides:** short, clinically reviewed explainers.
- **Voices:** stories written or recorded by Indian survivors.

**2. Topics, by journey stage and cancer type**

| Topic area                   | Example pieces                                                                                           |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Understanding your diagnosis | Reading your pathology report; what "stage" and "grade" mean; a glossary linked to the records assistant |
| Your body after surgery      | Wound and drain care; arm exercises after breast surgery; lymphoedema signs                              |
| Side effects                 | Fatigue; neuropathy; "chemo brain"; hair and skin; sleep                                                 |
| Food                         | Indian vegetarian protein; eating with a sore mouth (head and neck); myths ("sugar feeds cancer")        |
| Moving again                 | Why exercise helps; safe starts; phases explained                                                        |
| Mind and emotions            | Fear of recurrence; scan anxiety; body image; intimacy; talking to children                              |
| Practical life               | Returning to work; money and PM-JAY; insurance; travel                                                   |
| Family and caregivers        | How to help; caring for the carer                                                                        |
| Myths and facts (India)      | Alternative "cures", diet myths, stigma; evidence-based answers                                          |

**4. Trust and safety (essential for a health app)**

- **Medical review badge** on every guide: "Reviewed by [name, role], [date]", with sources listed.
- **Plain language:** aim for school-level reading; avoid jargon or explain it.
- **Stories are moderated and edited:** no treatment advice, no alternative-cure promotion. Add an
  editor's note where a story mentions a specific treatment. Use content notes for recurrence or loss.
- **Consent and privacy** for every story: written consent, identity choices, the right to remove it later.
- **No open comments at first:** reactions only ("this helped"), to avoid misinformation and distress.
- **Indian context first:** Indian foods, costs, schemes and languages, with Indian clinical reviewers.

**5. Where content comes from**

1. **Write our own** core guides with the clinical advisory board (Indian context).
2. **Adapt NCI text** where useful (free of copyright, credit NCI), localised and reviewed.
3. **Link out** to trusted sources: Tata Memorial, Indian Cancer Society, NCI, Macmillan.
4. **Partner** with survivor groups and hospital programmes (Pink Hope / Self V, Apollo CanWin,
   Manipal We Can, Anandi Sheroes, Indian Cancer Society) to feature stories with permission.
5. **Possible ambassadors:** well-known Indian survivors who already speak publicly about cancer
   could be approached for a launch story or foreword, with permission only.

**6. Where Learn lives in the app**

The tab bar is full (5 tabs). Options:

- **Option 1 (recommended):** merge **Calendar + Medication** into one **"Care"** tab (appointments and
  doses together), freeing a tab for **Learn**. Tabs: Home · Care · Learn · Records · Profile.
- **Option 2:** keep the tabs; add a "This week's read" card on Home plus a Learn entry inside Records
  ("Records & Learn").
- **Option 3:** Learn as a Home tile next to Meditate, Move, Games and Reflect (least prominent).
