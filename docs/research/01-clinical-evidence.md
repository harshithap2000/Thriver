# 01 – Clinical evidence

What the research says about exercise and nutrition for people after cancer treatment and surgery,
and what that means for Thriver's promise of "clinically validated" personalised plans.

> Confidence key: **High** = major guideline or systematic review. **Medium** = single trials or
> secondary summaries of guidelines. **Low** = commercial or unverified sources.
> Not medical advice: plans for real patients need sign-off from oncology clinicians.

## 1. The evidence base for exercise

### International guidelines

| Guideline                                                                                                                               | Key message                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Confidence                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **ACSM International Multidisciplinary Roundtable, 2019** (Campbell et al., _Med Sci Sports Exerc_; Schmitz et al., _CA Cancer J Clin_) | Exercise training and testing are generally safe for survivors; everyone should "avoid inactivity". Specific doses improve **anxiety, depression, fatigue, physical function and health-related quality of life**. Evidence is still uncertain for peripheral neuropathy and cognitive function. | High ([PubMed 31626055](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31626055/), [NATAM summary](https://natamcancer.org/Health-Info-Physical-Activity-ACSM))                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **ASCO guideline, 2022** (_J Clin Oncol_)                                                                                               | Oncology providers **should recommend regular aerobic and resistance exercise during curative-intent treatment**, and may recommend pre-surgery exercise for lung cancer surgery. Based on 52 systematic reviews and 23 RCTs, mainly breast, prostate, lung and colorectal cancer.               | High ([ASCO guideline](https://ace.asco.org/guidelines/169005), [Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/hematology-oncology/20220919/exercise-should-be-standard-part-of-cancer-care-during-treatment-asco-guideline-states), [Breastcancer.org](https://www.breastcancer.org/research-news/asco-issues-guidelines-on-exercise-diet-and-weight-management-during-cancer-treatment)) |

### The general "dose" (2019 roundtable)

Reported by several secondary sources; check exact wording against the original papers before using
it in-app or in marketing.

| Component              | Prescription                                                                                             |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Aerobic                | Moderate intensity, **≥30 min, ≥3 days/week**                                                            |
| Resistance             | **≥2 days/week**, ≥2 sets of 8–15 reps at about 60% of one-rep max                                       |
| Fatigue                | 30 min moderate aerobic, 3×/week, over a **12-week** programme                                           |
| Anxiety and depression | 30–60 min moderate aerobic 3×/week for 12 weeks, or combined aerobic + resistance 2×/week for 6–12 weeks |
| Change from 2010       | Minimum aerobic target lowered to about **90 min/week** (from 150)                                       |

Sources (Medium): [Breastcancer.org](https://www.breastcancer.org/research-news/updated-exercise-guidelines-for-cancer),
[CURE](https://www.curetoday.com/view/new-exercise-guidelines-for-cancer-survivors),
[University of Alberta](https://ualberta.ca/folio/2019/10/exercise-may-reduce-risk-of-cancer-recurrence-and-improve-survival-rates.html),
[Society of Behavioral Medicine](https://sbm.org/healthy-living/prevention-survival-and-recovery-from-cancer-the-case-for-exercise).

### Around surgery: prehabilitation and recovery

- **Prehabilitation** (exercise before surgery) of **1–4 weeks** cut hospital stays by about **1.8 days**
  in a Lancaster University analysis (Lambert et al., _Annals of Surgery_); even one week can help.
  ([Lancaster University](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/news/cancer-patients-discharged-from-hospital-sooner-following-preparation-for-surgery)) — Medium
- For colorectal surgery, a meta-analysis found prehab **improves functional capacity** and may reduce
  complications, but did not shorten stays; programmes over 3 weeks trended better.
  ([PMC9349170](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9349170/)) — High
- Typical trial design: start about 25 days before surgery and **continue 8 weeks after**; the prehab
  group walked further at 2 months post-surgery (McGill colorectal trial, via
  [NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/health-shots/2014/10/29/359343261/patients-do-better-after-surgery-if-they-do-prehab-first)). — Medium
- ERAS (Enhanced Recovery After Surgery) guidelines include prehab, but evidence levels are generally
  low to moderate. No consensus on timing or length (5 days to 6 months in pancreatic surgery).
- **Gap:** this round found **no general, evidence-based day-by-day post-operative exercise timeline**.
  Early post-op activity (wound healing, lifting limits, drains) is set by surgical protocols at each
  hospital.

### Example: breast cancer surgery and lymphoedema

- Progressive resistance training (PRT) after breast cancer surgery **does not increase lymphoedema
  risk** compared with restricted activity, when symptoms are monitored (Cochrane, moderate-quality
  evidence). ([Cochrane CD009765](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD009765_interventions-preventing-lymphoedema-swelling-arm-after-breast-cancer-treatment)) — High
- PRT also **does not prevent** lymphoedema in the first year (RCT of 158 women after axillary
  dissection), but improves physical function and quality of life. Authors recommend **initial
  physiotherapist supervision**. ([WCPT abstract](https://world.physio/congress-proceeding/effect-progressive-resistance-training-lymphoedema-development-after-breast)) — Medium
- One study in the Cochrane review found more reported pain at 3 and 6 months in the training group.

**Takeaway:** the old advice to "protect the arm" has largely been replaced by gradual, monitored
strength training. That is exactly the kind of nuanced, cancer-specific guidance Thriver can deliver.

## 2. Safety rules (what the app must respect)

| Situation                     | Guidance                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Confidence                |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Before starting               | Exercise is generally safe, but **medical clearance** is advised in certain situations (2019 roundtable).                                                                                                                                                                                                   | High                      |
| Bone metastases               | Physician clearance first; avoid high impact (jumping), spinal bending and twisting; prefer walking or water exercise; seek care for new severe pain. ([Cancer Care Ontario](https://www.cancercareontario.ca/sites/ccocancercare/files/assets/H-PSOProgramExerciseConsiderationsSpecificToCancerType.pdf)) | Medium                    |
| Low white cells (neutropenia) | Infection risk: avoid gyms, pools and public training spaces until counts recover; clean shared equipment. ([Cancer Council NSW](https://www.cancercouncil.com.au/cancer-information/living-well/exercise-cancer/treatment-side-effects-and-exercise/side-effects-that-need-extra-care/))                   | Medium                    |
| Low platelets                 | Thresholds vary: roughly <50,000/µL avoid vigorous/resistance work, <20,000/µL light functional activity only. ([Santa Mina safety guide](https://cancerexercisetoolkit.trekeducation.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/36/2021/01/Santa_Mina_Safety-Reference-Guide-for-Exercise-for-People-with-Cancer.pdf))   | Medium (sources disagree) |
| Lymphoedema risk              | Long-term resistance programme 2–3×/week, initially supervised.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Medium                    |
| Recent surgery                | Follow the surgical team's restrictions. No general evidence-based timeline found.                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Gap                       |

**Product implication:** Thriver needs a **safety gate** before plans unlock: cancer type, surgery
type and date, current treatment, known bone metastases, lymphoedema, ostomy, neuropathy, recent
blood counts, plus clinician clearance. It also needs **red-flag symptom checks** that stop exercise
and route to care.

## 3. Nutrition

| Source                         | Key message                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Confidence                              |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| **WCRF**                       | All survivors should follow the general Cancer Prevention Recommendations after acute treatment, "as far as possible": a largely plant-based diet, limited red and processed meat, limited alcohol, a healthy weight, regular activity. WCRF has only reviewed survivor-specific evidence for **breast cancer** so far. ([WCRF survivors](https://www.wcrf.org/dietandcancer/cancer-survivors), [during & after cancer](https://www.wcrf.org/dietandcancer/recommendations/during-after-cancer)) | High (general), Low (survivor-specific) |
| **ASCO 2022**                  | **No neutropenic diets** to prevent infection during treatment. No recommendation for or against ketogenic, low-fat, fasting, supplements or functional foods, because evidence is lacking.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | High                                    |
| **ESPEN/ESMO** (protein)       | Protein of at least **1.0–1.2 g/kg/day**, up to 1.5–2.0 g/kg when depleted. Sources disagree on the exact floor. Low muscle mass predicts poorer treatment tolerance and survival. ([Nutricia booklet citing ESPEN/ESMO](https://nutricia.com.au/adult/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/2024/12/Oncology-Booklet_V5.pdf), [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00520-023-08218-z))                                                                                                    | Medium                                  |
| **ASPEN 2026** (head and neck) | 1.2–1.5 g/kg/day protein during any treatment. ([Guideline Central](https://www.guidelinecentral.com/insights/mar-2026-aspen-nutritionadultsheadneckcancer-guideline-spotlight/))                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Medium                                  |

**India note:** most nutrition evidence comes from Western diets. Indian meal plans (vegetarian
protein sources, regional cuisines, fasting practices, tobacco and alcohol cessation, betel/areca
for oral cancer survivors) need an **Indian onco-dietitian** to adapt them.

## 4. What this means for Thriver's personalisation promise

The pitch says: _"clinically validated exercise protocols and nutritional plans tailored precisely to
an individual's specific cancer type, surgical history, and exact timeline of days elapsed since
their last procedure."_

- **Defensible:** guideline-based exercise and nutrition for survivors; tailoring by cancer type and
  side effects (lymphoedema, bone metastases, fatigue, neuropathy); progression over 8–12+ weeks.
- **Not yet defensible as stated:** "clinically validated" **day-by-day** post-surgical protocols
  generated by AI. Those timelines belong to surgical teams, and an AI generating them would face
  medical-device regulation (see [04](04-mvp-and-regulation.md)).

**Suggested model:** a **phase-based protocol library** written and signed off by an advisory board
(oncology physiotherapist, onco-dietitian, oncologist). Each phase unlocks only with clinician
clearance, for example:

1. _Early recovery:_ follow the hospital's instructions; gentle mobility, breathing, walking.
2. _Return to activity:_ after the surgical team clears the patient; low-intensity aerobic work plus
   range of motion.
3. _Rebuild:_ the guideline dose above, with cancer-specific adaptations.
4. _Thrive:_ long-term maintenance and prevention.

AI then **personalises within the approved library**: choosing, ordering, adjusting intensity from
symptoms and adherence, and explaining. It never invents clinical protocols. This is safer, more
credible to hospitals and investors, and easier to keep on the right side of regulation.

## 5. Evidence for digital programmes

- 12 RCTs (1,450 survivors): activity-tracker interventions **may improve adherence**, but data are
  limited. Adherence sometimes topped 70%. ([JNCCN 2019, via Futurity](https://www.futurity.org/activity-trackers-cancer-exercise-1997082-2/)) — Medium
- 23 RCTs of mHealth diet and activity programmes: feasible and acceptable, but participants were
  mostly white (median 84%) and college-educated, which is **poor evidence for Indian users**.
  ([Cancers 2022](https://doaj.org/article/614e2c2c47e94de59d6f51391ab2e4fe)) — Medium
- 21 digital home-exercise programmes (1,578 women): programmes **following guidelines and with coaches**
  did better than those without. ([Health Informatics J.](https://research.vinuni.edu.vn/research-output/digital-home-based-post-treatment-exercise-interventions-for-female-cancer-survivors-a-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis/)) — Medium
- Hong Kong "THRIVE" app for breast cancer survivors: 16-week feasibility study, 74% adherence and
  no dropouts. Activity did not significantly rise, but some psychological measures improved.
  ([HKU](https://hub.hku.hk/handle/10722/355226)) — Medium
- A 200-person pragmatic RCT of a cancer-exercise app started in May 2025 (NCT06791018).

**Implication:** human coaching (an oncology physio or exercise specialist) plus guideline-based
content beats an app alone. That supports a **"specialist access"** feature and a hybrid business
model.

## 6. India-specific research gaps

- A systematic review found only **13 Indian exercise-oncology studies (1991–2013)**, mostly low to
  moderate quality and limited to breast and head and neck cancers.
  ([Manipal](https://researcher.manipal.edu/en/publications/exercise-based-interventions-for-cancer-survivors-in-india-a-syst/)) — Medium
- **Opportunity:** partnering with an Indian cancer centre on a pilot study would give Thriver its own
  Indian evidence. That is a strong differentiator and fundable through research grants.

## Open questions for clinical advisers

1. Which cancer types to launch with? Breast cancer has by far the strongest exercise evidence and is
   the most common cancer in Indian women.
2. What clearance workflow is realistic with Indian oncologists and surgeons (paper form, WhatsApp,
   in-app link)?
3. Which red-flag symptoms should stop a session and prompt contact with the care team?
4. How to adapt for head and neck cancer survivors (common in India): swallowing, nutrition, neck
   and shoulder mobility?
