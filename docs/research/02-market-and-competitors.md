**Most common cancers** (NCRP 2020): men: lung, mouth, stomach, oesophagus. Women: **breast and
cervix**. Breast cancer is rising; cervical cancer is declining. About a third of cancers are
tobacco-related, and India has high oral-cavity cancer rates.
([NCRP 2020 brief](https://www.ncdirindia.org/All_Reports/Report_2020/Brf_2020/NCRPReport2020_Brief_write_up.pdf))

→ **Launch focus suggestion:** breast cancer (largest group, strongest exercise evidence, often
younger and smartphone-using patients), then head and neck (an India-specific need with heavy rehab
requirements), then colorectal and others.

### The survivorship and rehabilitation gap

- Cancer rehab in India is **"underdeveloped and underutilised"**: gaps in infrastructure, access,
  awareness and trained staff, and limited funding.
  ([World J Clin Oncol 2025](https://www.wjgnet.com/2218-4333/full/v16/i10/110531.htm)) — Medium
- Survivorship care mostly happens inside routine oncology clinics (91% of 59 paediatric centres).
  Distance, lack of knowledge and inadequate facilities are the main barriers to follow-up.
  ([IJMPO](https://ijmpo.org/assets/articles/2024/pdf/s-0043-1761262.pdf)) — Medium
- Palliative care: 7–10 million Indians need it and **under 4% have access**. Median travel time to the
  nearest centre is about 2 hours, with huge state differences (Kerala near-universal; Bihar, Odisha
  and Madhya Pradesh poor).
  ([ecancermedicalscience 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12904676/)) — High

### Financial toxicity (critical for the business model)

- In 12,148 patients across seven Indian centres, **catastrophic health expenditure** rose from 23.6%
  (direct costs) to **59.4%** with indirect costs included; impoverishment rose from 14.1% to 27%.
  Average **direct out-of-pocket spend was ₹3.31 lakh (US$4,171) per patient per year**.
  ([Frontiers in Public Health 2023](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2023.1065737/full)) — High
- A smaller study (226 patients) found 90.1% catastrophic spending at six months.
  ([PMC10316647](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10316647/))
- The burden **continues after treatment ends**, which is exactly Thriver's phase.

**Implication:** a ₹1,000+/month consumer subscription will exclude most patients. Free or very
low-cost access for patients, paid for by institutions, is more realistic (see [03](03-business-model-and-funding.md)).

### Public coverage: Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY

- Covers cancer under **200+ packages / 500+ procedures** across medical, surgical and radiation oncology
  and palliative medicine.
  ([Rajya Sabha, March 2025](https://rsdebate.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/759019/1/PQ_267_11032025_U1392_p407_p408.pdf))
- About **68.4 lakh admissions worth ₹13,161 crore** were authorised for cancer packages up to February 2025;
  over 75% of these treatments were in rural areas.
  ([IANS](https://ianslive.in/rural-areas-avail-over-75-pc-cancer-treatments-under-pmjay-minister--20250319133116))
- PM-JAY pays for treatment, **not survivorship or rehab apps**. That is a gap, and a possible future
  policy angle.

### Positioning gap

No Indian product combines **(1)** guideline-based, clinician-gated exercise rehab, **(2)** Indian
nutrition, **(3)** a health-records vault with AI explanation, and **(4)** family and community support,
for the **post-treatment** phase. Thriver's current app already demonstrates (3) and (4) and a basic (1).

## 4. Name check: Thrivors

A US company called **Thrivors** ("The Cancer Care Companion", St. Paul, Minnesota, founded about 2015)
offers cancer exercise, nutrition and mental-wellness programmes, which is very close to Thriver in both
name and concept. ([Nancy's List](https://nancyslist.org/2020/11/02/thrivors-the-cancer-care-companion/), [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/thrivors))
Academic apps named **THRIVE** also exist.

**Actions before investing in the brand:**

1. Search the IP India trademark registry (classes 9, 42 and 44 for software and health services) and
   USPTO/WIPO for "Thriver" and similar marks.
2. Check app-store names and domains.
3. Consider a distinctive variant or tagline-led brand if conflicts appear.

## 5. Geriatric and palliative angle (initial notes)

- Need is large (7–10 million needing palliative care, under 4% access), but palliative care is a different
  product from survivorship wellness: symptom control, caregiver support, end-of-life planning.
- **Recommendation:** keep the MVP focused on curative-intent survivors. Treat palliative and geriatric
  oncology as a later expansion or a partnership (for example with Pallium India or CanSupport).
  More research is in the backlog.
